Blue Horizon

Interactive Installation · May–June 2025

A multi-medium interactive installation that visualizes the impact of rising sea levels along Boston's Esplanade. The project translates long-term climate data into immersive, time-based experiences.

Client Class Project — Northeastern University
Role Designer
Timeframe May – June 2025 (4 weeks)
Tools
Figma Procreate Photoshop

Problem

Climate change feels invisible until it's too late

Climate change often feels abstract and distant. Rising sea levels are difficult to visualize in familiar, everyday environments, which limits emotional engagement and urgency.

  • Climate data feels intangible and disconnected from daily life
  • Lack of emotional connection to long-term environmental change
  • Limited public awareness of localized impact in Boston

Solution

Three installations, three timescales

We designed a three-part interactive installation that immerses users in the Esplanade's present and imagined futures through AR, sculpture, and experiential storytelling. The experience is structured around time:

  • 1 hour AR tour
  • 10 year eroding zinc boat
  • 100 year bronze sculpture that slowly submerges as sea levels rise

Key Design Decisions

UXR Takeaways

01

Focus on real places, not statistics

People connected more with specific spots like a bench or walking path than with large climate numbers. So we centered each experience around familiar Esplanade landmarks.

02

Use time to create emotion

Instead of only showing the final outcome, we designed the installations to change slowly over time. This helps visitors feel the impact little by little.

03

Make it easy to access

We used QR codes instead of building a separate app. This made the AR experience simple and accessible to anyone with a phone.

Iteration

From sketch to final concept

Early sketches explored a single-medium installation before we committed to the three-timescale structure.

#FBFAF8 — Off-white
#B4EEE7 — Seafoam
#3DAFB5 — Teal
#69A297 — Sage
#332C23 — Dark Brown

Cool teal and seafoam reference the water itself, while the dark brown grounds the installation in the soil and materiality of the Esplanade.

Final Solution & Demo

The three installations

01 — 1 Hour

AR Simulation

QR codes placed along the Esplanade trigger AR overlays showing water level changes from 1975 to projected 2100 futures.

02 — 10 Years

Zinc Boat

A floating zinc boat that slowly erodes over a decade until it breaks apart and sinks into the river.

03 — 100 Years

Bronze Sculpture

Four bronze figures playing in shallow water that slowly appear to drown as the sea level rises across the century.
